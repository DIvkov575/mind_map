Hyperbolic geometry is a non-Euclidean geometry with constant negative curvature. In the upper-half-plane model,
$$
\mathbb H=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb R^2:y>0\},
\qquad
ds^2=c\,\frac{dx^2+dy^2}{y^2}.
$$
The curvature is $-1/c$.

Consequences of the factor $1/y^2$:

- the same coordinate displacement is shorter at larger $y$
- geodesics are vertical lines and semicircles orthogonal to the boundary $y=0$
- the boundary $y=0$ is infinitely far away in the metric
- horizontal lines are horocycles rather than geodesics

For the univariate Gaussian family, set $x=\mu/\sqrt2$ and $y=\sigma$. Its [[Fisher Information|Fisher metric]]
$$
ds^2=\frac{d\mu^2+2d\sigma^2}{\sigma^2}
=2\,\frac{dx^2+dy^2}{y^2}
$$
therefore has curvature $-1/2$. This is why the shortest statistical path between distributions need not be a straight line in parameter coordinates. See [[Information Geometry]].
