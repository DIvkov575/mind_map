Ethylene-vinyl acetate (EVA) is a flexible copolymer of ethylene and vinyl acetate. Its chain combines:

- ethylene units: $\mathrm{-CH_2-CH_2-}$
- vinyl-acetate units: $\mathrm{-CH_2-CH(OCOCH_3)-}$

The ethylene-rich backbone provides flexibility and low-temperature toughness. Increasing vinyl-acetate content introduces polar [[Functional Groups|ester groups]], generally increasing softness, tack, and compatibility with polar surfaces.

“EVA glue” can refer to materially different products:

- **Hot-melt EVA:** applied molten and solidifies while cooling.
- **EVA dispersion:** polymer particles in water; hardens as water evaporates.
- **Solvent-based EVA:** polymer dissolved in a volatile carrier.
- **Heat-seal coating:** a thin factory-applied layer activated by heat and pressure.

These formulations are not interchangeable. For a thin [[PET]] or [[Mylar]] seam, a purpose-designed heat-seal coating gives more uniform thickness than a glue-gun hot melt; actual bond strength still depends on formulation, surface treatment, pressure, temperature, and dwell time. See [[Glue]].
