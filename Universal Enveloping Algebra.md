The associative algebra $U(\mathfrak g)$ built from a [[Lie Algebra]] $\mathfrak g$ by turning the [[Lie Bracket]] into a commutator:
$$
XY-YX=[X,Y]\quad\text{inside }U(\mathfrak g).
$$
It is the smallest associative algebra containing $\mathfrak g$, with the Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt (PBW) basis of ordered monomials in a basis of $\mathfrak g$.

**Why it is useful**

- **Representations of $\mathfrak g$ = modules over $U(\mathfrak g)$.** All of [[Representation Theory]] becomes ordinary (associative) module theory.
- It is where products of generators live — so the [[Casimir Operator]] (central, invariant) is an element of $U(\mathfrak g)$, not of $\mathfrak g$ itself.
- Realized concretely, generators become **first-order differential operators**, so $U(\mathfrak g)$ is the natural home for the operator/PDE view of symmetry (Laplacians, [[Homogeneous Space]] analysis).
