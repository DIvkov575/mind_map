
 A conformer is one possible three-dimensional arrangement of a molecule produced by
 rotation around single bonds.

 Conformers have:
 - The same atoms.
 - The same covalent bonds.
 - Different torsion angles.
 - Different three-dimensional coordinates.
 - Potentially different energies.

 For example, rotation around a carbon-carbon bond can produce different conformers
 without breaking any bonds.

 For a [[Protein]], a conformer is one member of the set of structures accessible to the same
 amino acid sequence. Examples include open and closed [[Enzyme]] states, alternative
 side-chain rotamers, and different loop configurations.

 A conformational distribution follows [[Boltzmann Distribution]]:
$$p(x) = \frac{\exp\left(-E(x)/(k_B T)\right)}{Z}$$
 where:
$x = \text{molecular coordinates}$          and           $E(x) = \text{energy of conformer } x$

$$Z = \int \exp\left(-E(x)/(k_B T)\right)\,dx$$

 A protein structure model normally predicts one or a few likely conformers. A molecular
 dynamics or generative ensemble model attempts to represent a distribution over
 conformers.
