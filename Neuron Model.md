A mathematical abstraction of a biological neuron — reduce the cell to a state variable and a simple update rule, discarding most of the biophysics, so the resulting network can be analyzed or simulated. Different levels of abstraction trade biological fidelity for tractability:

- **Perceptron / McCulloch-Pitts unit**: weighted sum of inputs through a (nonlinear) activation — the ancestor of the artificial "neuron" used throughout modern deep learning, but really a caricature of a real neuron's actual dynamics.
- **Hopfield Network**: a network of binary units with symmetric weights that settle into a stable, low-energy configuration — the same energy-minimization framework as [[Boltzmann Machines]] (main difference: deterministic settling instead of Boltzmann's stochastic Gibbs sampling toward a thermal equilibrium distribution).
- **Hebbian Learning**: "neurons that fire together, wire together" — a local weight-update rule (increase $w_{ij}$ when $s_i$ and $s_j$ are co-active) rather than a model of the neuron itself, but it's the learning rule Boltzmann Machines' own weight update ($\langle s_i s_j\rangle_{\text{data}} - \langle s_i s_j\rangle_{\text{model}}$) generalizes — see [[Boltzmann Machines]]'s training section.
- **Leaky integrate-and-fire / spiking models**: keep an actual continuous membrane-potential state that leaks over time and fires a discrete spike at threshold — closer to real neurophysiology, the level of abstraction used when [[Oscillators|coupled-oscillator]] models of brain rhythms need an underlying spiking substrate rather than an abstract phase variable.

All of these treat the neuron as the unit being modeled — as opposed to [[Cerebral Cortex]], which is about the anatomical structure many neurons are organized into, or [[fsaverage]], which is about representing that structure's geometry as a mesh.
