A multiphase liquid is a liquid system that contains two or more distinct liquid phases that do not fully mix with each other.
Fluid fractions let us describe this mathematically without tracking every microscopic interface. Instead of saying "this tiny region is 73% water and 27% air by volume," we assign a phase fraction $\alpha(x,t)$ that varies over space and time.

Of particular interest are multiphase fluids with continuum of phases (or [[Generalized Flows]]), introduced by Y. Brenier
