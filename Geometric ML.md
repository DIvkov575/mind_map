











### Leveraging Representation Theory — why work in the algebra
[[Representation Theory]], [[Lie Groups]], [[Lie Algebra]]

#### The ladder of inductive bias
- Raw: features are plain coordinates
    - symmetry has to be learned from data or augmentation, with no guarantee, so it is sample-hungry and can silently break
- [[Groups|Group]] + [[Representation Theory|representation]]: features are typed by [[Irreducable Representation (irrep)|irreps]] and layers are intertwiners
    - this gives *exact* [[Equivariance]], which is the payoff
    - but it only works when the group is compact and known: it leans on [[Wigner-D matrices]], [[Clebsch–Gordan Coefficients]], [[Peter–Weyl Theorem|Peter–Weyl]], and [[Haar Measure]] averaging, all of which assume that nice structure
- [[Lie Algebra|Algebra]]: work with the generators (the infinitesimal transformations) rather than finished group elements
    - preferred whenever one of the advantages below applies

#### Why the algebra helps
- Equivariance turns into a finite linear check
    - the group condition $f(\rho(g)x)=\rho(g)f(x)$ has to hold for *every* $g$, and a Lie group has uncountably many elements, so you can never test them directly
    - the two group-level escapes are both expensive: either hard-wire only provably-equivariant building blocks, which is restrictive, or average over the group with [[Haar Measure]], which is an integral you must actually be able to compute
    - differentiate the condition at the identity along a generator $X$ and it collapses to the linear relation $\nabla f(x)\cdot d\rho(X)\,x = d\rho(X)\,f(x)$
    - a connected group is generated by $\exp$ of its algebra, so satisfying this for the $\dim\mathfrak g$ basis generators already forces full equivariance — SE(3) becomes $6$ linear equations instead of an integral over a 6-dimensional group
    - the advantage: an uncheckable continuous condition becomes ordinary linear algebra you can either enforce exactly or add as a penalty
- Non-compact groups leave the group toolkit with nothing to stand on
    - for Lorentz, affine, or $SL_n(\mathbb R)$ the [[Haar Measure]] has infinite volume, so the averaging integral diverges and cannot build invariants
    - these groups also have no finite-dimensional unitary reps ([[Peter–Weyl Theorem]] fails), so the tidy finite irrep list simply does not exist
    - the algebra is untouched by this: it is still a finite-dimensional vector space with a bracket, and the infinitesimal check above needs no integration, so it is often the *only* option
- You can search for the symmetry instead of assuming it
    - a candidate symmetry is just generator matrices plus structure constants, constrained by the [[Jacobi Identity]], and all of that is differentiable, so you can gradient-descend to *discover* the symmetry
    - "the space of all groups" has no comparable smooth parametrization, so this discovery is only possible at the algebra level
- Invariants and couplings fall out of the algebra structure
    - [[Clebsch–Gordan Coefficients]] are generated by [[Ladder Operators]], so you can build the equivariant tensor-product nonlinearity even for groups whose [[Wigner-D matrices]] are not tabulated
    - the [[Casimir Operator]] is a ready-made invariant that labels the irrep, so it drops straight into a pooling or readout layer
- For dynamics, the generator *is* the model
    - a rate law $\dot z = A z$ with $A\in\mathfrak g$ learns the generator directly, and the [[Exponential Map (Lie Theory)|exponential map]] turns it into an exact flow $z(t)=\exp(At)z_0$, avoiding numerical-integrator drift
    - by Noether each generator is a conserved quantity, so conservation is built in rather than hoped for
    - see [[Neural ODE]], [[Koopman operator]]

#### In practice
- LieConv (Finzi, Welling, Wilson, 2020) and LieTransformer (Hutchinson et al., 2021) build equivariant layers for arbitrary Lie groups exactly this way
    - they need no closed-form global group action: lift points into the algebra via the log map, enforce the bracket-linear constraint there, then exponentiate back
    - the algebra is where the constraint is actually linear algebra

#### Where groups are still fine, and the caveats
- for a compact, simply-connected group the algebra and group carry the same information, so the gain is ease of enforcing and learning, not extra expressive power
- the infinitesimal check only implies symmetry on the *connected* component, so reflections and parity (chirality) are invisible and must be added by hand — see [[Exponential Map (Lie Theory)]]
- a soft generator penalty gives only approximate equivariance, whereas hard group-equivariant layers are exact
