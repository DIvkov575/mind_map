[[Bacteria]] with a natural intracellular magnetic chain that senses and orients the organism along magnetic fields.
- Magnetotactic organisms produce intracellular structures called magnetosomes — membrane-bound crystals of magnetic minerals (typically magnetite, Fe₃O₄, or greigite, Fe₃S₄).
- This allows the organism to align with Earth's magnetic field and navigate efficiently, usually to reach optimal oxygen concentration zones.
- Magnetotaxis = movement guided by magnetic fields.

The magnetosome chain only orients the cell — actual locomotion still comes from ordinary bacterial [[Motility Structures]] (typically flagella); magnetotaxis biases *which direction* that motility drives the cell toward, it doesn't replace the motility itself. This combination — a genetically/chemically engineered magnetic-sensing or magnetic-steering system riding on a natural motility apparatus — is the same design pattern exploited artificially in [[Magnetic Biohybrids]].
