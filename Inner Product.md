An inner product is a rule that assigns a scalar $\langle u,v\rangle$ to two vectors and therefore defines lengths, angles, and orthogonality.

On a real vector space $V$, it satisfies

1. bilinearity: $\langle au+bv,w\rangle=a\langle u,w\rangle+b\langle v,w\rangle$
2. symmetry: $\langle u,v\rangle=\langle v,u\rangle$
3. positive definiteness: $\langle v,v\rangle>0$ for every $v\neq0$

It induces
$$
\|v\|=\sqrt{\langle v,v\rangle},
\qquad
\cos\angle(u,v)=\frac{\langle u,v\rangle}{\|u\|\|v\|}.
$$
Vectors are orthogonal when $\langle u,v\rangle=0$.

An inner product is a positive-definite symmetric [[Bilinear Form]]. A [[Riemannian metric]] assigns one smoothly varying inner product to every [[Tangent Space]] of a manifold. In [[Information Geometry]], that choice is normally the [[Fisher Information|Fisher-Rao inner product]].
