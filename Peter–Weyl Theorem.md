For a compact [[Groups|group]] $G$, the matrix coefficients of the finite-dimensional [[Irreducable Representation (irrep)|irreps]] form a complete orthonormal basis of $L^2(G,\mu)$ (with [[Haar Measure]] $\mu$). Equivalently the regular representation decomposes as
$$
L^2(G)\;\cong\;\bigoplus_{\pi\in\hat G} V_\pi^{\oplus \dim V_\pi},
$$
each irrep appearing with multiplicity equal to its dimension.

**Consequences**

- It is the group generalization of Fourier analysis: [[Wigner-D matrices]] are the "Fourier basis" on $SO(3)$.
- It justifies why compact-group equivariant networks can be built from a *finite* list of irreps — the irreps are all finite-dimensional and they span everything.

**Boundary**

Requires compactness (finite Haar volume). For non-compact groups it fails — no such finite-dimensional unitary decomposition — which is exactly where the [[Lie Algebra|algebra]]/infinitesimal approach takes over. See [[Hilbert Space]], [[Representation Theory]].
