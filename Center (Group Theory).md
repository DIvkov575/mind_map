If $G$ is a [[Groups|group]], the center $Z(G)$ is the set of elements that commute with everything:
$$
Z(G) = \{z \in G : zg = gz \ \forall g \in G\}
$$

- $Z(G)$ is always a normal subgroup of $G$ (it's fixed by every conjugation, since $z$ commutes with every $g$)
- $G$ is abelian iff $Z(G) = G$ — the center measures exactly how far $G$ is from being commutative
- for $G = GL_n(F)$ (see [[Groups]]'s Matrix/Linear Groups section), $Z(G)$ is the nonzero scalar matrices $\{cI : c \in F^\times\}$
- the Lie-algebra analogue is $Z(L) = \{z \in L : [z,x] = 0\ \forall x \in L\}$ — see [[Lie Algebra]]'s Properties section
