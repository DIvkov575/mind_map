The [[Weight (Representation Theory)|weights]] of the adjoint representation of a semisimple [[Lie Algebra]]: diagonalize the [[Cartan Subalgebra]] acting on $\mathfrak g$ itself, and the nonzero eigenvalues $\alpha$ are the **roots**,
$$
[H,E_\alpha]=\alpha(H)\,E_\alpha .
$$
Each root $\alpha$ carries a [[Ladder Operators|ladder operator]] pair $E_{\pm\alpha}$.

**Why it is central**

- The geometry of the roots (their angles and relative lengths, encoded in Dynkin diagrams) *classifies* the simple Lie algebras — a discrete, finite fingerprint of a continuous object.
- Roots + Cartan generate the whole algebra, and the ordering of roots defines "highest weight", hence every [[Irreducable Representation (irrep)|irrep]].

So the chain **Cartan $\to$ roots $\to$ ladder operators $\to$ irreps / [[Clebsch–Gordan Coefficients]]** is the structural core that makes algebra-level representation theory computable.
