A one-parameter group (or one-parameter subgroup) is a continuous [[Groups|group]] [[Homomorphism]]
$$
\phi:\mathbb R\to G
$$
from the real line, viewed as the [[Additive group]] $(\mathbb R,+)$, into a topological group $G$. It is a curve through the identity that respects addition of the parameter:
$$
\phi(s)\phi(t)=\phi(s+t),\qquad \phi(0)=e.
$$
It packages a single "direction of motion" in $G$ into a trajectory indexed continuously by time.

**Generator**

When $G$ is a [[Lie Groups|Lie group]], differentiating at $t=0$ gives an element of the [[Lie Algebra]],
$$
X=\dot\phi(0)\in\mathfrak g=T_eG,
$$
the infinitesimal transformation the flow generates. Conversely every $X\in\mathfrak g$ integrates back to a one-parameter subgroup through the exponential map:
$$
\phi(t)=\exp(tX).
$$
So one-parameter subgroups and Lie-algebra elements are in bijection: the algebra is the space of *velocities*, the one-parameter groups are the *paths*. For matrix groups this is $\phi(t)=e^{tX}$, the matrix exponential.

**As a flow**

The [[Group Actions|action]] of a one-parameter group on a space is a [[Flow]]. A smooth vector field on a [[Smooth Manifold]] induces, at least locally, a one-parameter group of [[Diffeomorphisms]] carrying points along its integral curves; the flow condition $\varphi_{s}\circ\varphi_{t}=\varphi_{s+t}$ is exactly the homomorphism law above.

**Examples**

- $G=\mathbb R^n$: $\phi(t)=tv$ — uniform translation along $v$.
- $G=SO(2)$: $\phi(t)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\cos t&-\sin t\\\sin t&\cos t\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ — rotation at unit angular speed.
- $G=\mathrm{Aff}^+(1)$: $\phi(t)=e^{tD}$ generated by the dilation $D$ — pure rescaling; see [[Affine Group]].

A one-parameter group need not be injective (e.g. rotation is periodic), so its image can be a circle rather than a line.
