Mylar is a trade name commonly used for biaxially oriented [[PET]] (BoPET) film. During manufacture, PET film is stretched along two in-plane axes and heat-set, aligning polymer chains and improving tensile strength, dimensional stability, clarity, and electrical insulation.

The repeating PET unit is:

$$[-\mathrm{O{-}CH_2{-}CH_2{-}O{-}C(O){-}C_6H_4{-}C(O)}-]_n.$$

BoPET can be metallized by vapor-depositing a thin aluminum layer. The metal makes the film reflective and reduces gas and moisture permeability; emergency blankets use this aluminized construction.

Bare BoPET is not inherently heat-sealable. Packaging laminates therefore add a sealant layer or coating, such as polyethylene or a compatible [[Ethylene-Vinyl Acetate (EVA)|EVA]] formulation. See [[Glue]] for joint behavior.
