A [[Groups|group]] with finitely many elements — its order $|G|$ is finite.

- among the simplest nontrivial examples: the [[Dihedral Group (D_2n)]] — symmetries (rotations + reflections) of a regular $n$-gon, order $2n$
- every finite group embeds into some [[Symmetric Group (S_n)]] via its own action on itself (Cayley's theorem — see [[Groups]]), and also into some [[Linear Group GL(n)]] via its regular representation, so both classes are "universal" for finite groups
- classification of finite groups is a deep area on its own (simple groups, the classification theorem, Sylow theorems for prime-power structure)

Contrast with continuous/infinite groups like [[Lie Groups]], where the group also carries a smooth manifold structure.
