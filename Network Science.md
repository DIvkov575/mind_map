study of systems represented as graphs — nodes (parts) + edges (interactions) — where the topology of *who connects to whom* determines collective behavior: spreading, synchronization, collapse. Coalesced ~1998–2005 when large-scale data (internet, social webs, metabolism) met graph theory + statistical physics.

Founders' canon: Milgram small-world experiment (1967) · [[Watts–Strogatz Model|Watts–Strogatz]] (1998) · [[Barabási–Albert Model|Barabási–Albert]] preferential attachment (1999) · PageRank (1998) · Albert–Jeong–Barabási robustness (2000).

**Structure of the field**

*1. topology — anatomy of real networks*
- [[Small-World Networks]] — high clustering + short path lengths; Watts–Strogatz = lattice rewired with prob $p$
- [[Scale-Free Networks]] — heavy-tailed degree $P(k) \sim k^{-\gamma}$, $\gamma \approx 3$ across internet, metabolism, social webs; generated by [[Preferential Attachment]] (rich get richer)
- clustering & transitivity, [[Motifs]], [[Community Detection]] (modularity, Louvain), core–periphery, assortativity
- modern: temporal, multiplex, spatial networks

*2. processes on networks — dynamics over topology*
- [[Epidemic Models]] SIR/SIS: epidemic threshold $\tau_c \approx 1/\lambda_{\max}(A)$ — the spectral radius alone sets outbreak vs die-out
- [[Percolation]] & robustness: scale-free nets absorb random failure, shatter under targeted hub attack
- cascading failures: power grids, financial contagion, ecological collapse
- [[Kuramoto Model|synchronization]] on graphs — coupling topology sets sync threshold
- diffusion, random walks, navigability (Kleinberg 2000)

*3. inference — networks from data*
- [[Centrality]]: degree, betweenness, eigenvector, [[PageRank]], k-core — *which node matters*
- [[Link Prediction]], community detection as statistical inference (SBMs)
- edge inference from time series: [[Granger Causality]], [[Transfer Entropy]]
- null models: [[Erdős–Rényi]] $G(n,p)$, [[Configuration Model]]

*4. theory core*
- random graph theory, branching processes
- [[Spectral Graph Theory]] — adjacency/Laplacian spectra control dynamics on the graph
- generating-function formalism (Newman) — exact percolation & threshold results
- statistical mechanics of disordered systems

**Objectives**
1. universal structural signatures — small-world + heavy tails recur in every domain
2. topology → dynamics: predict thresholds, cascade sizes, sync from the graph alone
3. robustness/fragility & intervention — vaccinate hubs, harden key links
4. inference from partial, noisy data
5. engineered networks — routing, overlays, intervention design
