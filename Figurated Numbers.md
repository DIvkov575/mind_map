Numbers representable as a regular geometric arrangement of dots — a discrete, combinatorial analogue of continuous geometric shapes.

- classic examples: triangular numbers ($1,3,6,10,\dots$), square numbers ($1,4,9,16,\dots$), pentagonal numbers, etc.
- [[Centered Polygonal Number]]s are the sub-class built around a central dot with successive polygonal "rings" — each ring has a fixed number of sides, and each side of a given ring has one more dot than the corresponding side of the previous ring
- generating-function / recurrence relations for each family connect back to elementary combinatorics (binomial coefficients generate the "non-centered" polygonal numbers)
