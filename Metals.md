Metallic solids: atoms arranged in a crystal [[Lattice]] (typically a [[Bravais Lattice]] — e.g. face-centered cubic, body-centered cubic), held together by delocalized electrons shared across the whole structure rather than fixed bonds between specific atom pairs — the source of metals' characteristic conductivity and ductility.

**Annealing (metallurgical sense)**
Heat a metal, then cool it slowly. This gives atoms enough thermal energy to escape metastable positions and settle into a lower-energy, more ordered lattice configuration — reducing crystal defects (dislocations, internal stress) that a fast quench would freeze in place.

[[Annealing|Simulated annealing]] in statistics/ML is a direct algorithmic borrowing of this exact idea: gradually lower a "temperature" parameter so a sampling/optimization process settles into a low-energy (high-probability) state instead of getting stuck in a poor local configuration — the physical process and the algorithm solve the same shape of problem (escaping metastable states via gradual cooling) in two completely different domains.
