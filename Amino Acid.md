The building block of proteins — a small molecule with an amino ([[Functional Groups|–NH₂]]) group, a carboxyl ([[Functional Groups|–COOH]]) group, and a side chain (R group) that varies between the 20 standard amino acids and determines that residue's chemistry — the side chain's own reactivity/charge is itself governed by whatever [[Functional Groups]] it carries (e.g. the amine that makes Lysine's side chain basic). The specific bond angles within a side chain come from its atoms' [[Electron Arrangements]].

**Charge, relevant to [[Salt Bridge|salt bridges]]**
- Positively charged (basic) side chains: Lysine (Lys/K), Arginine (Arg/R), Histidine (His/H)
- Negatively charged (acidic) side chains: Aspartate (Asp/D), Glutamate (Glu/E)
- A [[Salt Bridge]] forms between an oppositely-charged pair, e.g. Lys+/Arg+ with Asp−/Glu−; [[Lys Bridge Swap|swapping Lys for Arg]] in such a pair changes the interaction's geometry and strength without changing its charge sign.

Amino acids link end-to-end (peptide bonds) into a chain — the sequence that folds into a [[Protein Structure]].
