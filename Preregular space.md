A [[Topological Space]] $X$ is **preregular** (also called an **R1 space**) if any two topologically distinguishable points can be separated by disjoint neighbourhoods.

- "topologically distinguishable" means at least one of the two points has an open neighbourhood not containing the other — weaker than requiring the points to actually be different in every way ([[Hausdorff Space|Hausdorff]] requires *any* two distinct points to be separable, preregularity only requires it for pairs that are already distinguishable)
- sits below Hausdorff ($T_2$) and above the plain $T_1$/$T_0$ separation axioms in the usual hierarchy of increasingly strong separation conditions on a topological space
- every Hausdorff space is automatically preregular (distinct points are always distinguishable in a Hausdorff space), but not conversely — a preregular space can have topologically indistinguishable-yet-distinct points that Hausdorff would forbid
