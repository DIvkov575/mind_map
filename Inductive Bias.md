==The set of assumptions a machine learning model uses to predict outputs for unseen data, allowing it to generalize beyond training examples==. It limits the hypothesis space, preferring simpler or structured solutions (e.g., smoothness, linearity) to prevent [[Fitting|overfitting]]. Without it, learning new, unseen data is impossible.

- **architectural inductive bias**: baked into the model class itself before any training happens — e.g. convolution's translation-equivariance assumption, or an RNN/[[Autoregressive Models]]'s assumption that order matters
- **[[Regularization]] as inductive bias**: rather than restricting the hypothesis space structurally, penalize complexity directly during training — [[Ridge regularization (L2)]] encodes "prefer small weights," [[Lasso regularization (L1)]] encodes "prefer sparse weights." Both are inductive biases expressed as a training-time penalty instead of an architectural constraint
- the [[Fitting|bias-variance tradeoff]] is the quantitative version of this: too little inductive bias (an unconstrained hypothesis space) → high variance, overfitting; too much (an overly restrictive one) → high bias, underfitting. Choosing an inductive bias is choosing where on that tradeoff a model sits, before it ever sees data
